# What the test measures

The 25-hydroxyvitamin D test shows the body's vitamin D status from sunlight, food and supplements.

# Levels

According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, levels below 30 nmol/L (12 ng/mL) are linked with deficiency. Levels of 50 nmol/L (20 ng/mL) or more are adequate for most people. Levels above 125 nmol/L (50 ng/mL) may be linked with possible harm. To convert, 1 ng/mL equals about 2.5 nmol/L.

# Good to know

Vitamin D often changes with the seasons and is usually lower in late winter. Whether low vitamin D causes tiredness is not settled. Taking high amounts without medical advice can be harmful, so talk with your clinician first.
