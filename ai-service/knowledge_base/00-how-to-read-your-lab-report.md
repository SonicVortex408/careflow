# How to read your lab report

Each line of a lab report usually shows the test name, your result, the unit, and a reference range. The reference range is the span of values seen in most healthy people tested by that lab. About 1 in 20 healthy people will have a result just outside the range, so a single value outside it does not always mean something is wrong.

Different labs use different units. For example, free T4 can be reported in ng/dL or pmol/L. PolyMarker converts every value to one standard unit so results from different labs can be compared.

Ranges can differ by lab, age, sex and the test method. Your clinician looks at your results together with your symptoms, history and medicines.

# Flags

Many labs print H (high) or L (low) next to a result outside their range. Some print an asterisk. A flag is a prompt to look closer, not a diagnosis.
