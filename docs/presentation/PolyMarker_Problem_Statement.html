The problem statement

Lab reports are hard to read, judged one value at a time, against ranges built for healthy people, not for when symptoms begin.

01

Unreadable formats

PDFs, scans and photos with different lab wordings and units.
02

Judged in isolation

Each value is compared on its own with a population "normal" range.
03

Symptoms disconnected

Fatigue, brain fog and hair loss are rarely linked to the numbers.

Patients with "normal" values who still feel unwell get no explanation, and clinicians have no scalable way to see patterns across many markers and many patients.

Sub-problem 01

Unstructured, heterogeneous data

Results arrive as text PDFs, scans and photos from different labs, with different test names, units (ng/mL vs nmol/L), formats and errors. Analysis isn't possible until they're read accurately and normalised to standard codes and units.

Sub-problem 02

One value at a time misses the pattern

Thyroid hormones, antibodies, vitamin D, B12, ferritin, magnesium and zinc interact. A patient can have several "borderline" values that together explain their symptoms. The traditional one-value-at-a-time reading misses that.

Sub-problem 04

Symptoms aren't linked to lab data

Patient-reported outcomes (tiredness, brain fog, hair loss) are rarely captured in a structured way or modelled against biomarkers. Without that, there is no evidence-based "your pattern is associated with these symptoms."

Today: no structured link.

Sub-problem 05

Patients can't safely understand their results

Raw reports are hard to read, and an unsupervised AI that diagnoses or suggests doses would be unsafe. Every explanation must pass four tests:

✓
Readable: grade 8 or below
✓
Traceable to evidence
✓
Free of diagnosis or prescribing
✓
Signed off by a clinician
✕
"You have Hashimoto's"
✕
"Take 50,000 IU of vitamin D"

The AI is never the last step.

From problem to solution

How PolyMarker answers each part

Unstructured data
OCR + layout-tolerant parser + LOINC/unit normaliser + data-quality checks; Spark/DuckDB ETL over a 50,000-patient data lake
Multi-marker patterns
Joint feature vectors, K-Means / GMM / DBSCAN clustering, knowledge graph of marker–condition–symptom links
Functional ranges
Spline-based functional-band discovery, compared against hidden ground truth
Symptom correlation
Calibrated XGBoost risk models per symptom with SHAP explanations (AUROC 0 for tiredness)
Safe patient understanding
GraphRAG assistant with deterministic guardrails as the final check, plus mandatory clinician review before any result is shown

The constraint we state up front

All population data is synthetic

The system demonstrates the method. It does not make clinical claims about real thresholds. This is validity threat R2 in the evaluation report.

Symptoms were generated from marker values. Recovering those thresholds proves the pipeline finds structure that is there. It is not evidence that such thresholds exist in real people.
